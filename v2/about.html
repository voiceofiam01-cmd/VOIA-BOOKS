We are a small publishing house in Lagos. We do not publish gurus. We do not publish courses with "certified" in the name. We do not publish people who have not lived the thing they are writing about.

We publish short, honest books on the things nobody teaches you — how to stop running out of money before the month ends; how to sleep when your brain holds meetings at 3am; how to love someone without disappearing into the spiral; how to grow old without becoming a burden; what the oldest question asks of you after every answer has disappointed you.

Our books are short enough to finish in a weekend. They are long enough to be honest. We do not use hype. We do not manufacture scarcity. We do not write "as seen on" when nothing has seen us. If a book is bad, we will tell you before you buy it. If a book is good, we do not need to shout.

We are here for the reader who is tired of being shouted at. The reader who has bought the system, enrolled in the program, followed the formula, and is still a person at the end of it. The reader who knows that self-help cannot save you and spiritual platitudes cannot feed you and nobody's framework replaces the small daily work of becoming more honest with yourself.

We do not promise enlightenment. We promise plain language, real names, authors who have stood where the reader is standing, and a refund that is not a test.

— The Editors, Voice of I AM

House Rules

How we publish.

I.

Plain language

We do not write "utilize" when "use" does the work. We do not borrow language from psychology, theology, or business to make something ordinary sound important. If a sentence needs to be read twice, we rewrite it.

II.

Authors who have lived it

Every author writes from inside the room they are describing. The short books were written from inside the late-twenties leak, the 3am scroll, the overthinking, the first paycheck that ran out on the 18th. The long inquiries are written from Mgbowo, not from a seminar room. We do not commission books from people who researched the subject for six weeks.

III.

No false promises

We do not promise you will be a millionaire, healed in 30 days, anxiety-free, completely secure, or certain about God. We promise you a system, a serious question asked honestly, and a place to start. The rest is yours.

IV.

Nigerian first

Nigerian editions are written in Nigerian English, priced in Naira, built around Nigerian life — generator noise, POS alerts, black tax, Mgbowo twins, the 18th of the month. Global editions do not flatten Nigeria out of the writing; they translate what must be translated and leave the rest of the world to catch up.

V.

No hype, no scarcity

We do not run fake countdowns. We do not tell you "only three copies left" when there are infinite PDFs. We do not pretend a price is going up at midnight. A good book lasts; it does not need a timer.

VI.

Straight refund

Buy a book. Read it. Try the thing it asks you to try. If it doesn't speak to you, message us on WhatsApp or email. We refund you, same day, no argument, no quiz. A small press cannot afford unhappy readers.

VII.

Reading as fast as reading

Our books are designed to be read on a phone without feeling like a blog post. No walls of grey text. No walls of beige inspiration. Typesetting, spacing, and typography matter because attention is the thing being asked for.

VIII.

We do not sell your email

If you give it to us for a free chapter, you get letters. You do not get sold, rented, or passed to a partner. Unsubscribe is one click. We do not believe in a 97-email sequence wearing you down.

M

Munachimso E. Nwagbara

Longform Collection · Mgbowo, Enugu State · $9.99 – $16

Munachimso writes the long works under his own name. He is the author of The Watching Mind, the It-Twice Trilogy, the novel Nothing to Understand, Everything to Know, the death-shelf inquiries anchored by Why Must We Die?, and the spiritual-inquiry works anchored by Who Is God?

He writes from Mgbowo, Enugu State. His work draws on Igbo philosophical traditions, Christian theology, Western philosophy, oral tradition, and ordinary lived experience without subordinating any one to the others. He does not claim to have solved ultimate reality. He claims to have written books that do not lie about how hard the honest questions are.
