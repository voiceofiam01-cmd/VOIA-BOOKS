A note on how to read it

  1. Read the Author's Note and Method Note first. They are short and they tell you exactly what kind of book you are holding — and what kind it refuses to be.
  2. Read in order if you can. The book builds. But if the question is loudest somewhere particular, start there. The method note tells you how.
  3. Keep the Evidence-Status Key near you while you read. It is how you'll know what kind of claim you are being asked to trust on any given page.
  4. Argue with it in the margins. This book expects to be argued with. A reader who nods through the whole thing has not read it.
