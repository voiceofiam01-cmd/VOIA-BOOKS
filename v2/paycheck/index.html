Why budgets fail

It's not the small chops.
It's never been the small chops.

Every advice column tells you the same thing: track every kobo, cut the small chops, stop buying shawarma. You've tried it. It doesn't stick.

Because the problem isn't your self-control. The problem is that you have four or five big, slow leaks you can't see — your data plan, one subscription you forgot, that "urgent 2k" message you never track, the way fuel + generator silently eat ₦30k+ before you notice.

Stop blaming your willpower. Start reading the system.

Questions

The ones you're already asking.

Will this work if I earn less than ₦300k — or more than ₦1M?

Yes. The plan is built around your number — fixed costs ÷ income — not your salary size. If you earn ₦180k or ₦1.8M, the same four-word framework applies; only the numbers change. The irregular-income chapter covers freelancers, drivers and business owners too.

Is this another "give up small chops" book?

No. You can eat small chops. The problem is almost never ₦1,200 at a time. It's the ₦40k–₦140k in silent monthly leaks you can't see. Chapter 3 finds them.

Do I need an app or a spreadsheet?

Neither. You'll need your banking app, a pen, and about 45 minutes the first night. After that, the Drill runs once a month in 20 minutes.

What if I have debt?

Chapter 7 is written exactly for you. You don't pay extra toward debt until leaks are plugged and a shield is up. There's an order. Skipping it is why debt keeps coming back.

What about people depending on me — family, children, extended?

That's not a side note here. The children-on-board section in chapter 9 is the most-read part of the book. It gives you a real framework for when your income carries other people.
