Before You Leave.

He did not write this as a Christian trying to win you back. He did not write it as a dibia trying to recruit you. He did not write it as an atheist trying to convince you there is no Chukwu. He wrote it as someone who has lived inside the questions.

He was born into a Christian home. He inherited prayers, hymns, scriptures, and a sincere faith that carried his family through hard years. He also inherited names, language, stories, and a village that existed before those hymns arrived. For a long time he did not know how to hold both without betraying one.

This book is not Who Is God? That book is the longer investigation. This one comes before that. It is for the moment when inherited answers stop working — when you are afraid to say aloud that you are questioning; when you love your mother but can no longer pray exactly as she taught you; when you leave one authority and discover that another person is already waiting to sell you certainty.

If Who Is God? is the map of the forest, this is the compass you use before entering it.

Do not use it to insult the people who gave you a beginning. Use it to become responsible for what you do with that beginning.

About the author

He wrote this from inside the question.

M

Munachimso E. Nwagbara is from Mgbowo, Enugu. He was born into a Christian home and has spent much of his adult life in the terrain this book describes — between inherited Christianity and the inherited traditions that existed before it, between what his mother taught him and what his own eyes have seen.

He will tell you plainly that he is not writing from a settled position outside the question. He is writing from inside it, as a fellow traveller, with the same vulnerabilities to recruitment, to romance, to easy certainty, that every seeker has. The book is the compass he wished he had been handed when the answers first stopped working.

Read this before reading Who Is God? (L09) or any of the longer books. It is short on purpose — because at the moment the answers stop working, you do not need a 100,000-word treatise. You need a compass.

Questions

The ones before you buy.

Does this book tell me to leave Christianity / return to tradition?

No. It tells you how to investigate so that whatever you end up doing is chosen rather than defaulted into or reacted into. The author has seen people return with clearer eyes and people leave with clearer eyes, and considers both outcomes preferable to unexamined acceptance or unexamined reaction.

Will this hurt my relationship with my parents?

Chapter 2 ("Your Mother Was Not the Enemy") is devoted specifically to this question. The book teaches you to separate love from agreement, to honour the people who gave you a beginning without being trapped by every line on their map. It does not guarantee every relationship will survive your honesty — some relationships are built on control, not love — but it gives you tools for the ones that can.

I've already left the church. Is this book still for me?

Especially. Chapter 9 ("You Can Leave and Still Be Lost") is written for people who left one structure only to walk into another one — a different priest, a different payment, a different certainty, the same cage. If you have relocated without investigating, this book will show you.

Do I need to read this before Who Is God?

Recommended but not required. This compass is for the moment before you enter the forest. The long book is the forest itself. If you are currently in crisis, in doubt, in the vulnerable moment between one authority and the next, start here.

Aren't journal prompts a bit self-help?

They are. They are also one of the oldest technologies we have for thinking something through in private rather than in the presence of someone who wants to recruit you. The prompts are short, concrete, and designed to produce answers you can look back at in a year.

How long is it really?

~12,000 words — about 60 pages. You can read it in an afternoon. The prompts will take you longer, which is the point.
