The diagnosis

It's not the coffee.
It never was.

Every personal-finance guru blames your $4 latte. You've heard it. You've cut the coffee. You've restarted in January. And you still end the month wondering where it went.

Because the problem isn't $4 at a time. It's three or four big, slow leaks — subscriptions you forgot, a recurring transfer, the way you pay insurance, the quiet cost of being broke (overdraft fees, buying in small quantities, late penalties) — that siphon $300–$1,500/month before you notice.

You don't lack willpower. You lack a map.

Four words run the whole plan.

LeaksStop the money you don't see leaving.
BucketsStop letting salary land in one pile.
BufferBuild one month of runway — without a side hustle.
DrillA 20-minute monthly routine that makes it stick.

That's the whole book. No apps. No shame. No exotic investments. Just four words, applied in order.

Questions

The ones you're already asking.

Is this only for people on low incomes?

No. The system works on any income because it's about ratios, not amounts. Early readers have used it on $2,000/month and $14,000/month. The chapter on the Scarcity Tax is often more important for higher earners — silent leaks scale up with income.

Do I need a budgeting app?

No. You'll need your bank statement and about 45 minutes the first night. After that, the Drill runs once a month in 20 minutes, with nothing to install.

Is this US-specific?

No. The global edition uses neutral examples (dollars as a unit). Chapter 9 covers irregular income, dependents, and country-specific banking regardless of where you are.

How does the 30-day refund work?

What if I have debt?

Chapter 7 is built for exactly that. You don't throw extra money at debt until leaks are plugged and a small buffer is up. There's an order. Skipping it is why debt comes back.
