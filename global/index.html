    The problemIt's not the coffee. It never was.

    If you earn $3,000, $5,000, even $15,000 a month and your account is empty by the 18th, you are not bad with money. You are not lazy. You are playing a game that was built to take your money back.

    ADP Research's "People at Work 2024" global survey asked more than 38,000 workers across 34 countries whether they were living paycheck to paycheck — fifty-seven percent said yes. A separate analysis of US consumer finances by PYMNTS / LendingClub has repeatedly found that a large share of high earners (including people earning over $100,000/year) also report living paycheck to paycheck. The point isn't the exact number; the point is that when the income is bigger, the system that quietly eats it gets bigger too. ADP source → PYMNTS source →

    "This is not a poor-people problem.
    It's a design problem.
    And design can be rebuilt." — From the foreword

    You've tried the apps. You've tried tracking receipts. You've tried "no spending months" that collapse by Wednesday. They all fail for the same reason: they start with your spending, when the damage was already done before you bought anything you actually wanted.

    • 01

      Subscriptions you forgot

      $14 here, $11 there, a gym you last entered in January. Eleven small charges can be $180 a month — and none of them feel like a decision.

    • 02

      Convenience charges

      The $22 meal that becomes $34 after delivery, service fee and tip. Card surcharges, ATM fees, foreign transaction fees. Together, a second rent.

    • 03

      Buy now, pay later

      Four or five small instalments you agreed to across months and forgot to track. The app calls it flexible. Your bank calls it four more bills.

    • 04

      The fixed-cost flood

      Rent, transport, insurance, childcare, phone, loans, school fees, family support — all automatic, all invisible, all before you bought anything fun.

    Fixed costs are the flood. The small treats are a teaspoon. Any advice that starts with the teaspoon isn't solving your problem — it's just making you feel guilty while you drown.

    The whole systemFour words. That's it.

    Every chapter, every worksheet, every script, every template in this book translates back to four words. You can read the whole thing in about two hours, and you'll be using those four words on your next payday.

    Leaks

    Money that leaves without a decision

    Subscriptions, fees, renewals, payment plans, delivery charges, bank bites. Find your three biggest leaks and plug them. Most people recover $150–$500 a month here.

    Buckets

    A split that happens before you spend

    On payday, before you touch anything, split into four buckets: Bills (60–70%), Life (20–25%), Buffer (5–10%), Future You (5%). Then Bucket 2 becomes the only number your eyes can see.

    Buffer

    Your first real shield

    $1,000. The size of one ordinary emergency — a phone screen, a car battery, a clinic bill. Built in 30 to 60 days. Stored in a separate account, no card. Money leaves only for an emergency you defined in advance, in calm.

    Drill

    8 minutes. Every payday. Forever.

    Eight minutes to run the split, check the buckets, move the Buffer, pay one bill ahead. That's the whole recurring system. Willpower runs out by 9pm. Rules don't.

    QuestionsThe ones you're already asking.

    Is this another "stop buying coffee" budget book?

    No. Chapter 1 proves that coffee is not the problem — fixed costs and invisible leaks are. There is no suffering, no "track every penny," no shame. The small joy is explicitly protected.

    Does it work outside the US? I'm paid in [my currency].

    Yes. The Global Edition uses US dollars as the teaching currency only — every figure is an example for teaching the method, not a rule. There's a currency conversion table at the front covering US$, £, €, ₦, ₹, R (South Africa), KSh (Kenya), ₱ (Philippines), R$ (Brazil), C$, A$ and ¥. The percentages (60–70% bills, 20–25% life, 5–10% buffer, 5% future you) are the part that travels — they don't care what your money is called.

    Yes. The plan is about the day money lands, regardless of what day that is. Freelance, commission, gig and irregular-income readers have their own section (Section 10 in the Global Edition). You run the same 8-minute drill every time money hits your account.
