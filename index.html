Why this house existsMost life-advice books were not written for your life.

You are a salary earner staring at your account on the 18th. You are a daughter supporting a family at 28. You are a person in midlife watching the shape of things change. You are a worker on a Sunday evening, wondering how five more years of this can pass. You are someone carrying a question no one around you seems to be asking.

The big publishing houses don't write for you. The loud online coaches don't write in your accent. The people close to you love you, but they don't always have words for what you are feeling.

We try to write those books — the one you'd hand to a friend, with their actual struggle, in their actual language, under the actual conditions of this life. Sometimes short and direct. Sometimes long and slow. Always honest.

"We are not trying to sell you a new life. We are trying to help you meet the one you already have." — From the house editorial note

How we publishA few things we refuse to do.

Publishing has a set of habits we are not interested in inheriting. These are the promises a reader can hold us to.

— 01

No fake urgency.

No countdown timers, no "only 4 copies left," no manufactured scarcity. Buy when you need the book. The book will still be there.

— 02

No fake readers.

We don't invent reviews. If you see a reader quote, it's from a real person who gave permission to share it.

— 03

No guarantees.

We don't promise the book will make you rich, married, healed, or calm. It's a book. Results are your own.

— 04

No bait-and-switch.

Buy the book. Read the book. There is no $500 course hidden in the appendix. There is no funnel.

— 05

No shame.

We don't write at readers. We write from somewhere close to the problem. You are not the first person to be here.

— 06

No filler.

A short book is not a failed long book. We write until the idea is done, then stop. Real-You books stay short on purpose.
